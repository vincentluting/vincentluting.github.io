## The situation

My first real job after my studies was at Huawei in the Netherlands, as a marketing financial controller. Huawei was growing fast in Europe at the time, and it spent a lot on marketing and retail campaigns: more than $30 million.

Every quarter, headquarters wanted to know how that money would be spent. If the forecast was wrong, money sat unused in one place while another campaign ran short. When I started, the forecast was right about 82% of the time. <!-- TODO(Vincent): how was accuracy measured? -->

## The hard part

The information was spread out. Campaign plans lived with the marketing team, costs came in through finance, and results came from many channels, online and in shops. Putting it together was manual work, done in a hurry at the end of each quarter. <!-- TODO(Vincent): confirm. -->

It was also my first job in a large Chinese company in Europe. The rhythm of headquarters and the rhythm of the local market were very different, and I was in the middle.

## What I did

**I built a tool to track the return on every campaign.** Instead of collecting numbers by hand at the end of the quarter, I automated the analysis of what each campaign cost and what it brought in. <!-- TODO(Vincent): what did you build it with? Excel/VBA, SQL, something else? -->

**I used the results to forecast better.** Once we could see which types of campaigns delivered and how their costs usually developed, the forecasts could be based on facts instead of estimates.

**I reported to management in a way they could act on.** Every quarter I prepared budget allocation reports for C-level management: where the money went, what it brought, and where it should go next.

**I helped the marketing team learn from each campaign.** We looked at what worked online and in shops, and used that in the next campaigns. Engagement after campaigns went up by 15%.

## What came out of it

Forecast accuracy went from 82% to 96%. Management got clearer reports, and the marketing budget was spent with more confidence.

## What I would do differently

I was young and tried to solve everything with a better spreadsheet. Today I would spend more time with the marketing team first, to agree on what we measure and why. The tool was good, but the conversations it started were worth even more.

## Why this matters for the work I want to do next

Every project runs on money and time. I know how to read a budget, build a forecast and explain it to management. In a hardware project, that helps me keep costs in view and raise it early when the numbers start to drift.

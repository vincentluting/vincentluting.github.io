## The situation

Headquarters chose a new mail system, and every office had to move. <!-- TODO(Vincent): from which system to which? How many offices and roughly how many people? -->

On paper this is an IT task. In reality it is a people task. If mail stops working on a Monday morning, nobody cares how good the new system is.

I was asked to be the local project manager for Europe. HQ owned the technical side. My job was to make sure Europe was ready on the day.

## The hard part

Nobody was tracking, office by office, who was ready. "Ready" meant many small things: the account was set up, the person knew what would change, their phone was configured, shared mailboxes were moved, and they knew where to go if something broke. <!-- TODO(Vincent): confirm the list. -->

The date was fixed, so Europe could not simply ask for more time. <!-- TODO(Vincent): true? -->

## What I did

**I made readiness visible, office by office.** I built a simple tracker with one row per office and went through it regularly with the local contacts. It was not a clever tool, but it meant we always knew where the gaps were, and I could raise a risk early instead of on the last day. <!-- TODO(Vincent): how often did you review it, and who saw it? -->

**I rewrote the instructions for the people who would use them.** The HQ material was correct, but it was long and technical. I wrote two user manuals in plain language, with screenshots for every step. <!-- TODO(Vincent): what were the two manuals about exactly? -->

**I ran Q&A sessions before go-live.** People could ask anything, and I collected the questions. Questions that came up more than once went into the manuals. <!-- TODO(Vincent): true? -->

**I offered one-on-one help for the people who needed it most.** Some colleagues were nervous about the change, or had special setups. I helped them one by one and moved things over together. <!-- TODO(Vincent): in person, on calls, or both? -->

## What came out of it

Europe went live on the planned date. After the switch, there were zero complaints from users. There were questions, of course, but they were answered quickly because the answers were already written down.

Later, other regions asked for my manuals and used them for their own migration. The documentation outlived the project, which is what good documentation should do.
